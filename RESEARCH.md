## Six sub-genres, not one

**1. The .io arena.** Snake.io, Snaker.io, Worms Zone, Insatiable.io, Little Big Snake,
wormate.io. Slither.io's descendants: top-down 2D, free-angle steering, boost by ejecting
mass, you kill by making someone else's head hit your body. Instant respawn, no fail
screen worth the name. Monetisation is rewarded-video revive + skin gacha + battle pass.
Snake.io launched in 2016 and shipped an update ten days ago — a decade of live ops. This
lane is defended.

**2. The clash treadmill.** Snake Clash! is the biggest number on the board: 774k ratings
in three years against Snake.io's 473k in ten. Supercent's move was to bolt a persistent
**level number** onto the snake and a boss ladder onto the session — `Lv 182 Player` chasing
`BOSS Lv 1000` on a 01:23 timer. Same loop, hybrid-casual meta. This is where the growth is.

**3. The 3D arena.** Snake Rivals, Boas.io, Snake Master 3D. Same loop, third-person camera,
battle-royale framing. Boas.io's twist is genuinely good (you swallow a *city* — cars,
buildings, a whole block at a time). But 3D makes snake's core legibility problem worse:
you cannot see your own tail, and your own tail is the thing that kills you. Note Snake
Master 3D hasn't been touched since 2021.

**4. The mechanic remix.** Snake VS Block (599k), Snake Run Race (134k), SSSnaker (Habby).
Keep the idea that your body *is* your length, throw the grid away entirely. Length becomes
ammunition against numbered blocks, a runner's queue, or a bullet-hell shooter's HP and DPS
at once. Highest return on invention of anything here — Snake VS Block is one mechanic from
2017 still pulling updates.

**5. The untangle puzzle.** Tangled Snakes (87k), Wiggle Escape (37k), Snake Escape (16.5k
in ten months, 4.79★). The current gold rush. Zero real-time skill: snakes are knotted, you
tap them out in the right order. Snake is a *skin* over the unblock/tap-away puzzle template
that's eating the casual puzzle charts. Fast money, extremely crowded, and not really snake.

**6. The thinky grid puzzle.** Snake Puzzle: Slither to Eat (2.2k, 4.10★), Snaggle (23
ratings), and off-store the genre's masterpiece, Snakebird (85 Metascore). Deterministic,
turn-based, one correct solution. The highest critical regard and the smallest iOS audience
by two orders of magnitude.

**Plus the relic.** Snake '97 — a pixel-faithful Nokia 3310 emulation, complete with the
phone bezel and keypad, 4.73★, fifteen years old, still updated this February. Nostalgia is
a durable niche and it is already perfectly served. Don't go there.

## Controls: nobody has solved this

Four families in use:

- **Floating joystick + boost button** — Snake.io, Insatiable.io, SSSnaker. Most precise,
  costs a thumb's worth of screen, needs a handedness setting.
- **Drag-anywhere / follow-finger** — Worms Zone, Little Big Snake. Your finger occludes
  the exact area you're steering into.
- **Swipe-to-turn** — Snake Rivals classic, Snake VS Block. Lowest fidelity, highest
  accessibility, works one-handed.
- **Tap-to-act** — the untangle puzzles. No dexterity at all, which is the point.

The tell: Snake.io ships **four** control modes plus left/right-handed layouts and lets you
reposition the joystick and boost button. Ten years and 473k ratings in, the market leader
still can't pick one. Touch steering for snake is unsolved. That's an opening.

## The visual monoculture

Near-universal across the chart, and it makes the whole category look like one game:
saturated candy palettes; googly cartoon eyes on the snake head; segmented bead bodies;
hexagonal or dotted backgrounds; leaderboard top-right with a crown on #1; skins sorted
COMMON / RARE / EPIC / LEGEND / VIP. Screenshot copy is all-caps on a colour banner —
"EAT EVERYTHING!" / "GROW BIGGER!" / "KILL SNAKES!" / "BECOME THE BEST!". Every one of them.

The two exceptions look like different mediums: Snake '97's monochrome dot-matrix, and
SSSnaker's painterly dungeon art (Habby, who can afford it).

## Where the gap is

**1. Nobody has made a beautiful one.** The chart is candy-hypercasual or literal Nokia
nostalgia, with nothing in between. There is no Monument Valley, no Alto's Odyssey, no
Threes! of snake — no calm, crafted, aesthetically confident version. Snaggle reached for
it in 2018 and stopped shipping in 2019.

**2. The grid has been abandoned.** Everything modern is free-angle and analogue. The
original's real pleasure — planning three turns ahead on a lattice, the discrete *click* of
a turn landing — survives only in the tiny thinky-puzzle corner. A real-time snake that is
still grid-locked, with proper input buffering so queued turns always land, is not on this
chart.

**3. Length is only ever a score.** Almost everything treats length as monotonically
increasing. Snake VS Block is the one game that lets you *spend* it, and it's the biggest
remix on the board. A snake where shedding length is a deliberate move — to fit through a
gap, to pay a cost, to reset a board — is an untapped vein.

**4. Two players, one device.** For a lot of people the best memory of Snake 2 is infrared
two-player. Nothing on this chart does local same-device or same-room multiplayer.

## Open questions before we scope

1. **Platform** — native iOS (SwiftUI/SpriteKit), or web-first PWA on Vercel? Web is the
   faster path and matches the existing stack; native is the only route to the App Store
   charts above.
2. **Business** — is this a portfolio/craft piece, or is it meant to earn?
3. **Which gap** — the aesthetic one, the grid one, or the length-as-resource one? They
   point at very different games.
